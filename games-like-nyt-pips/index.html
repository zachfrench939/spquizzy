Games like NYT Pips

People who like a short domino puzzle in the morning often search for games like Pips. Pasta Pips is Spiquizzy's take on that kind of grid: a free daily board where you place dominoes on colored areas until every area matches its label.

Spiquizzy is an independent site. It is not The New York Times, it is not an official companion to any Times puzzle, and it does not publish Times answers, hints, or today's Times board. If you want that puzzle, play it where it is published. This page is about Pasta Pips, and about what you get if you want a similar ten-minute habit somewhere else.

What a board asks you to do

A Pasta Pips board is a grid split into colored regions. Each region has a plain-language label, such as "Add to 8" or "All same". You have a tray of dominoes. Tap one, then tap two squares that share a side. The two halves land on those squares. A green edge means that region is satisfied. A red edge means the region is full but the numbers do not match the label. You win when every region is right and every domino has a home.

That is the family resemblance people are usually looking for. Both styles of puzzle ask you to cover a colored map with dominoes so the regions come out true. The differences are the ones that matter if you are choosing a daily game.

What is different

Pasta Pips is free and needs no account. The board is dealt in your browser from the date, so everyone on the same local day gets the same puzzle. Easy, medium, and hard are three sizes of that day, not three unrelated games. A streak stays on this device. Leave halfway through and the same board is still there when you come back. The share card shows a time, a row of squares, and a rank. It does not show where the dominoes went, so posting a result does not spoil a friend.

The labels are written out. "Add to 8" means the dots in that color add up to 8. "All same" means every dot in that color shows the same number. You do not have to memorize a legend. The how to play Pasta Pips page walks through the same three steps the in-game help uses: pick up a domino, read the label, fill the board.

A useful way to start

Read every label before you commit a domino, because a region that looks open can still be the wrong total. Place the pairs that can only sit in one region first. If an edge turns red, lift the domino and try the other orientation. The two numbers on a domino can swap, so the first square you tap is the first half. None of that is today's answer. The board changes every day, and the work is reading the labels in front of you.

Pasta Pips sits next to five other free dailies. Sauce Pipes is a pipe-turning puzzle. Ravioli joins two halves into one word, and it is not Wordle. Slip slides a meatball one square at a time. Twirl draws paths between matching pictures. Even Bowls splits numbers so every bowl shows the same total. The free daily puzzle page describes the whole set, if you want the menu rather than this one game.

If you searched for a Pips-like game because you wanted another quiet grid, start with today's Pasta Pips board. The rules page is there when you want the steps written out, and the archive keeps older dates if you would rather practice than play today.
