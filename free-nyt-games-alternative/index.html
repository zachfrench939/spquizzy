SPIQUIZZY

A free daily puzzle site

A lot of people want a free daily puzzle they can open in a browser, finish, and leave. Spiquizzy is one of those sites. It is not The New York Times, it is not a login wall in front of anyone else's games, and it is not a place to look up answers from another paper. Nothing on this page, or in the games, reproduces a Times puzzle.

What you get is six original pasta puzzles, a new board every day, and no account. The date on your device picks the board. Someone across town who plays the same difficulty gets the same one. Easy, medium, and hard are three sizes, so a short game and a longer one can share a morning. Streaks and half-finished games stay in this browser. Close the tab and come back. The board is still the one you started.

The six games

Sauce Pipes is the game on the front door. You turn pipes until red sauce runs from the pot to the plate. It is a path puzzle: openings have to meet, and sauce only fills a pipe the pot can actually reach. The rules are three short steps.

Pasta Pips is the domino puzzle. Colored areas carry labels such as "Add to 8". You place each domino on two squares that share a side until every area matches its label. People who like Pips-style grids usually start here. The longer note on that comparison is the games like Pips page, and it is careful about what this game is not.

Ravioli is the word puzzle, and it is not Wordle. You tap two halves that join into one real word. There is no hidden five-letter guess and no letter coloring. The games like Wordle page says that at full length so a search for a Wordle-like game does not land on a false cousin.

Slip is a sliding puzzle. Tap a block, then an arrow, and it moves one square. The meatball has to reach the green exit. Twirl is a path puzzle of a different shape: drag from one picture to its match and cover every square. Even Bowls is the numbers puzzle. Tap a number, tap a bowl, and make every bowl show the same total. The rule on the board tells you the total, so you are not guessing what the word even means.

How a day works

You can treat the six as a set or as a menu. The archive keeps past dates, which is useful when you want a second board without waiting for midnight. Each game has a how-to page with the same steps the in-game help uses, plus a picture and a Play button. Share cards show a time, a rank, and a row of squares. They do not show the solution, so posting a result does not spoil a friend who has not played.

Spiquizzy is free to open. There is a single ad slot on a game's splash, and the home page is clear of it. Page counts, if your browser allows them, are anonymous: no cookie, no account id, and no record when Do Not Track is on. That is the whole tracking story. It is not a profile, and it is not required to play. The about page says the same thing in fewer lines: one board per local date, three difficulties, no account.

If you want a daily puzzle that lasts about as long as a coffee, pick a game and start. Sauce Pipes is the usual first plate. The other five are one tap away in the menu, and the written rules are one tap away from each help dialog.
